## The Three Prices of an ABS Challenge

- Content class: MODELED, CONCEPTUAL
- Claims: CTS11-C01, CTS11-C08
- Alt text: Conceptual diagram showing the three possible prices surrounding an ABS decision. Using pass as the zero baseline, a correct challenge gains immediate correction value V and retains the challenge unit, while a failed challenge leaves the call unchanged and loses future inventory value C. Passing an incorrect call has opportunity cost V; this is the same V, not an additional payoff. The CT-S1 Challenge Threshold equals C divided by V plus C. These modeled values are not player confidence.
- Caption: Figure 1. With pass as the zero baseline, the break-even comparison uses plus V for a correct challenge and minus C for a failed challenge. The pass card restates V as an opportunity cost; it is not an additional payoff. V and C are modeled quantities, not player confidence or an action recommendation.
- Prohibitions: No action recommendation; No empirical player-confidence value

## Same Situation. One Count Changes.

- Content class: MODELED
- Claims: CTS11-C05, CTS11-C08
- Alt text: Dot plot comparing the modeled CT-S1 reference for one-one and one-two counts in a fixed bottom-fifth state: offense, trailing by at least two runs, no outs, bases empty, and two challenge units. The one-one threshold is about 67 percent in development and confirmation, while the one-two threshold is about 60 percent. Development and confirmation are directly labeled. This was the only one of three prespecified ordinary comparisons to meet the published display rule. The values are not player confidence or a player-facing challenge rule, and the result is not a universal two-strike rule.
- Caption: Figure 2. In this fixed game state, modeled CT-S1 was lower at 1-2 than at 1-1 in the one prespecified ordinary comparison that met the published display rule. All eight conventions preserved the direction, not the magnitude; the thresholds are not player confidence and the result is not a universal two-strike rule.
- Prohibitions: No universal count rule; No challenge command

## One Reference. Two Kinds of Uncertainty.

- Content class: MODELED
- Claims: CTS11-C04, CTS11-C05, CTS11-C06
- Alt text: Interval plot for the one-one and one-two confirmation states. Thin navy lines with open circles show the modeled CT-S1 references and 95 percent conditional sampling intervals: about 64.9 to 69.7 percent at one-one and 57.4 to 62.6 percent at one-two. Separate thick gold bands show CT Model Spread across eight conventions: about 58.7 to 75.7 percent and 50.9 to 69.5 percent. Model spread is not a confidence interval, interval overlap is not a paired test, and no paired sampling interval was calculated for the count difference. These values are not player confidence or a player rule.
- Caption: Figure 3. Thin lines with open circles show 95 percent sampling intervals conditional on the frozen reference. Separate thick bands show disagreement across eight modeling conventions. CT Model Spread is not a confidence interval, and interval overlap is not a paired test. No paired sampling interval was calculated for the count difference; Figure 2 separately reports that the direction survived all eight conventions.
- Prohibitions: Do not merge the two uncertainty objects; No interval for the contrast difference

## Stable Through Time. Sensitive to Definition.

- Content class: MODELED
- Claims: CTS11-C03, CTS11-C04
- Alt text: Two-panel summary separated by a divider. The left panel shows absolute CT-S1 reference drift from development to confirmation: median 0.76 percentage points and 90th percentile 2.61 points across 22,584 supported rows. The right panel shows that about 98 percent of 22,428 complete rows in each period had material CT Model Spread, defined as wider than ten percentage points.
- Caption: Figure 4. Absolute development-to-confirmation drift and material CT Model Spread wider than ten percentage points answer different questions and use different denominators. Stability of one frozen reference does not establish agreement among modeling conventions.
- Prohibitions: Do not net stability against disagreement; Keep denominators separate
