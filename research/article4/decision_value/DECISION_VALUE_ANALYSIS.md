# Article 4 decision-value analysis — findings

Research evidence, not article prose. Method and definitions are in [METHODOLOGY.md](METHODOLOGY.md) and checks in [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md). Every number here is generated by `analyze.py` into `output/` (tables named per section). Four things are kept separate throughout:
* **what happened** (observed behavior);
* **what was knowable** (decision-time inputs);
* **what the model estimates** (cross-fitted expectations);
* **what happened afterward** (HINDSIGHT).

Statements are descriptive or associational. None is causal. No behavior is characterized as optimal, irrational, wasteful or loss-averse.

## 1. Resource-independent challenge propensity (`article4_resource_independent_recognizability.csv`)

* **Selected model.** The selected evidence-only model (P2) uses unsigned boundary distance, side and nearest edge. It has no inventory or challenge-history term.
* **Chronological performance.** On strictly earlier months (19,436 rows): ROC AUC 0.670, log loss 0.533, calibration slope 0.99, intercept +0.14, ECE 0.024. By decile, observed rates run from 12% to 52% against predictions of 9% to 48%. The model slightly under-predicts because challenge rates rose during the season.
* **With situation added (P4).** Count, outs and inning raise AUC to 0.732. It is not used as the evidence measure because count determines run value, which enters separately.
* **Against Article 3's model.** On the same 10,364 offense rows, the Article 3 temporal model scores AUC 0.737 vs 0.636 here. That model contains situation *and* challenges remaining, so the gap measures how much of Article 3's discrimination came from situation and inventory.
* **Effects.** Challenge odds rise steeply with distance (spline). Relative to the bottom edge, misses at the top edge have OR 0.55 (0.50–0.61) and inside/outside about 0.72–0.75. Offense odds are about half of defense odds (OR 0.49).
* **Missingness.** None in the predictors.
* This is a model of observed challenge behavior. It is not a measurement of what any player perceived.

## 2. Challenge success (`article4_challenge_success_model.csv`)

* **Base rate.** 9,485 official challenges; 53.8% succeeded (offense 48.7%, defense 58.0%).
* **Barely predictable from decision-time observables.** The selected model (unsigned distance × side + edge + terminal count) reaches chronological AUC **0.587** (log loss 0.679; a base-rate-only model would be about 0.690), calibration slope 0.78, ECE 0.015. Offense AUC 0.576, defense 0.572; monthly AUC 0.56–0.61. Cross-fitted probabilities cluster between 0.45 and 0.65. A coarse side-and-count model reaches 0.571.
* **Offense success is flat in distance.** It stays about 49–53% up to 3 inches from the edge and falls to 29% beyond 4 inches. Unsigned distance cannot tell a pitch well outside from one deep inside. Defense success rises from 53% to 67% with distance.
* **Interpretation.** Once the side of the line is withheld, the observable features carry little information about success. The information that separates successful from failed challenges is largely not in these fields; it is presumably the player's own perception, which is unobservable.
* **Public-information benchmark.** Among all 192,475 called pitches within 6 inches of the boundary, 11.3% were actually wrong. P(incorrect) falls from about 44% within half an inch to under 1% beyond 4 inches (chronological AUC 0.864, slope 1.02).
* **Three benchmarks for eligible opportunities.** Mean P(success) is 0.55 (primary, challenger-selected), 0.30 (public information) and 1.0 (oracle).

## 3. Gross immediate expected value (`article4_immediate_expected_value.csv`, fig02)

* **Distribution.** Gross EV = P(success) × correction value. Over 20,124 eligible opportunities: median **0.063 runs**, mean 0.082, P90 0.139, P99 0.336, max 1.02. Total 1,644.6 runs of EV against 3,097.7 runs of correction value.
* **Challenged vs unchallenged.** Median 0.072 vs 0.061; mean 0.101 vs 0.075.
* **By side.** Offense mean 0.075 (P(success) 0.52); defense 0.089 (0.58).
* **Inventory suppression after controlling for gross EV.** Challenge odds with one challenge left vs two, controlling for the gross-EV spline, the propensity logit, side, inning, score state and late-and-close: **OR 0.673 (0.618–0.733)**, essentially unchanged from 0.661 (0.606–0.720) in the exploratory model.

**Option value of one challenge** (expected runs recoverable, start of half-inning, 0 outs; home team top half shown, others in the table):

| inning | league-behavior use (95% CI) | use if EV ≥ 0.05 | use at every opportunity | marginal 2nd challenge (league behavior) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.080 (0.078–0.082) | 0.170 | 0.160 | 0.020 |

* **Perishable.** The option value of a single challenge declines steadily through the game, roughly to 0.1–0.2 of its first-inning value by the 9th. The decline is smooth and was not forced.
* **Second challenge.** A second challenge adds little: about 0.02 runs at the start and near zero after the 6th. Because successful challenges are retained, one challenge is usually enough to cover the future stream.
* **Policy dependence.** The value depends on the use policy by a factor of about 2. League-typical use recovers about half of what a simple EV ≥ 0.05 threshold would. These are benchmarks, not recommendations.
* **Extras.** The small rise in the 10th inning reflects pooled extra-inning states and the grant rule.

## 6. Spend vs preserve (`article4_spend_preserve.csv`, fig04, fig05, fig06)

**Preservation cost.** Preservation cost = P(fail) × marginal option value. Median: 0.014 runs with one challenge left and 0.004 with two. The highest benchmark cost (upper CI, most valuable policy) has a median of 0.042 and 0.033. The median gross EV is 0.062.

**Classification** of the 19,899 regulation-inning opportunities:

| class | n | share | challenged |
|---|---|---|---|
| immediate EV exceeds every preservation benchmark | 17,316 | 87.0% | 26.7% |
| overlapping | 2,530 | 12.7% | 14.5% |
| preservation exceeds immediate EV | 53 | 0.3% | 13.2% |

* **Answers to the questions.**
  1. Teams challenged 26.7% of opportunities whose estimated immediate value exceeded every preservation benchmark.
  2. They passed the other 73.3% (12,687 opportunities), which is **85.2% of all passes**.
  3. The rate in that class is 25.8% with one challenge left vs 27.1% with two.
  4. High-value opportunities are not disproportionately passed with one challenge left. At net value ≥ 0.35, the raw rates are 58% vs 60%.
* **How the class share depends on the information set.** The share of passes in the "immediate exceeds" class is 85% under the primary success model, 61% under public information, 77% under the Sprint 5 RE table, and 99.9% under the oracle. That passes often exceed the preservation benchmarks is robust. The exact share is not.
* **Scarcity after the full decision-value adjustment.** One vs two challenges, game-clustered:

| model | OR | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| M0 exploratory model A | 0.661 | 0.606–0.720 |
| M1 + gross EV + propensity | 0.673 | 0.618–0.733 |
| M2 + gross EV + preservation cost | 0.797 | 0.680–0.934 |
| M3 net decision value (EV − cost) + propensity | **0.728** | 0.667–0.796 |
| M4 = M3 + team fixed effects | 0.708 | 0.648–0.774 |

* **Size of the effect.** The original 0.66× association **attenuates but survives**. At equal net value, the predicted challenge rate with one challenge left is 4.9 to 7.2 points lower (ratio 0.79–0.87) at net values from 0 to 0.35 runs.
* **Preservation cost is associated with lower challenge rates.** In M2, OR 0.037 per 0.25 runs of cost, about 0.88× per 0.01 run. Including it moves the inventory OR from 0.67 to 0.80. The cost term absorbs part of the scarcity association, so part of the scarcity pattern lines up with the value lost if a challenge fails.
* **Raw rates.** Raw rates at equal net value are close across inventory (fig05). The adjusted gap appears after conditioning on inning and side, because one-challenge states are concentrated late when every rate is higher.
* **Threshold vs generalized reluctance.** No interaction: inventory × net value OR 1.07 (0.77–1.47); inventory × gross EV OR 1.02 (0.75–1.41). Nearly all sensitivity variants agree. A higher value threshold under scarcity would show up as a gap that shrinks as value rises. Instead the inventory-1 curve sits below the inventory-2 curve by a roughly constant odds ratio. In the near-boundary population not conditioned on correctness, the interaction is 0.85 (0.74–0.97): the gap *widens* with value, the opposite of a threshold. **The evidence favors a generalized downshift over a value threshold.**

## 7. After a failed challenge (`article4_post_failure.csv`, fig07)

In regulation innings, having one challenge **is** having failed once. The two cannot be separated. What can be examined is variation around that state.

| inventory state (regulation) | n | raw rate | adjusted OR vs "two left, no prior success" (net value, propensity, controls) |
|---|---|---|---|
| two left, no prior success | 9,378 | 23.8% | reference |
| two left after a prior success | 4,903 | 30.1% | **1.22 (1.12–1.34)** |
| one left, failure ≤3 PA ago | 661 | 22.2% | 0.78 (0.64–0.96) |
| one left, failure 4-12 PA ago | 1,406 | 23.0% | 0.88 (0.76–1.02) |
| one left, failure >12 PA ago | 3,551 | 23.2% | 0.78 (0.70–0.87) |
| one left from extra-inning grant (descriptive) | 89 | 32.6% | not modelled (extras only) |

* **Size of the post-failure association.** Against the no-prior-success baseline, the one-challenge association is about **0.78–0.88**, smaller than 0.73 against all two-challenge states. About half of the one-vs-two gap comes from the other side: two-challenge states that follow a *success* have elevated challenge odds (1.22). With team fixed effects: 0.75–0.85 and 1.20.
* **Within one-challenge states.**
  * Recency of the failure does not matter: OR 0.97 per log plate appearance (0.90–1.05).
  * Whether the failure was on the same side does not matter: 0.93 (0.79–1.11).
  * **When the player now deciding is the one who failed**, the odds are 0.72 (0.59–0.88).
* **The extra-inning grant group is not reluctant.** It is the only one-challenge state without a failure: 32.6% raw, n = 89, unadjusted.
* **Wording.** This is a **post-failure behavioral association** combined with a post-success association. Game-level heterogeneity is one alternative explanation, for example an umpire whose zone produces many challenges of both outcomes. Selection of which teams fail is another. Nothing here supports a psychological label.

## 8. Expiration (HINDSIGHT; `article4_expiration.csv`, fig08)

* **Final inventory.** Across 4,390 team-games: 2 left 28.3%, 1 left 46.6%, 0 left 25.1%.
* **74.9% of team-games end with at least one challenge unused**, and **51.2% of all challenge units** (4,529 of 8,844, grants included) expire unused.
* **Team-games ending unused** had on average 4.85 eligible incorrect calls, 3.61 unchallenged, 0.51 runs of correction value passed and 0.27 runs of gross EV passed. The median highest-value pass was 0.185 runs. 94.8% of them passed at least one opportunity classed "immediate exceeds preservation".
* **By stratum.**
  * Close games 74.8% vs 74.9%; home 74.0% vs away 75.7%.
  * Extra-inning games 83.7%, driven by grants.
  * Teams range from 57.9% to 85.7%.
* **Timing of the biggest pass.** Among team-games ending unused, it was spread across innings 1-8: 10-14% each, 5.7% in the 9th.
* **Overlap with classified passes.** Of the 12,830 passes classed "immediate exceeds preservation" (extras included), **80.5% occurred in team-games that ended with an unused challenge**. The base rate for all passes is 78.9%, and for passes worth ≥0.50 runs it is 79.9%.
* None of this shows an earlier decision was wrong. It quantifies how often the protected resource expired unused.

## 9. High-value early passes (`article4_high_value_early.csv`, fig09)

The 126 unchallenged innings-1-3 opportunities worth ≥0.50 runs:
* **Decision-time:** mean P(success) 0.46, gross EV 0.33 runs, preservation cost 0.013 runs. **All 126** are in the "immediate exceeds preservation" class under the primary model. 106 had two challenges, 20 had one, and 15.9% followed a prior failure.
* **Hindsight:** a later opportunity with higher correction value **or** higher gross EV appeared in 8.7%. The team challenged again later in 88.1%, and 75.4% ended the game with a challenge unused.
* Accounting for success probability leaves the 8.7% hindsight result unchanged. P(success) scales the passed opportunity and its later competitors alike and barely varies. The 0.50-run threshold is a descriptive cut, not a decision rule.

## 10. Braves case (`article4_braves_decision_value.json`, fig10)

**What happened.** Pitch 824887:3:7, Mets at Braves, 2026-08-10, top of the 1st. With one out, Bichette on first and a 3-2 count to Benge, ball four was called on a pitch 2.74 inches inside the zone near its top edge. No official challenge was recorded. Baty hit a grand slam three batters later, and the final was 8-5.

**Not in any source:** the steal attempt, the catcher's throw, any call at second, and Elder's hat-tap request.

**What was knowable** (recorded state only): unsigned distance 2.74 inches, nearest edge INSIDE, inventory 2, top of the 1st, one out.

**Model estimates:**
* Resource-independent challenge propensity 0.56.
* P(success) 0.53.
* Marginal option value of the second challenge 0.019 runs.
* Preservation cost 0.009 runs (range across benchmarks 0.008–0.054).

| counterfactual runner state | correction value | gross EV | net vs league-behavior cost | class |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| recorded / no runner action | 0.687 | 0.364 | 0.356 | immediate exceeds preservation |
| Bichette safe at second | 0.612 | 0.325 | 0.316 | immediate exceeds preservation |

**What happened afterward:** four more runs scored. That realized outcome is a different concept from the decision-time expected value of 0.33–0.49 runs, and it is not the cost of the missed correction.

## 11. Sensitivity and falsification (`article4_decision_value_sensitivity.csv`)

### Evidence AGAINST the emerging thesis ("scarcity reluctance may not be well targeted to decision-time value")

1. **The effect shrinks as the decision framework improves.** It goes from 0.66 to 0.73 (net value) and to 0.80 (EV and cost separately). The preservation cost enters with the expected sign. Some of the "scarcity" pattern is consistent with the lost option value of a challenge.
2. **The effect is not purely scarcity.** Against teams that have not yet had a success, the one-challenge association is 0.78–0.88. Much of the one-vs-two gap comes from *elevated* challenging after a success (1.22). The pattern fits outcome-dependent, game-level challenge activity at least as well as it fits resource scarcity.
3. **Offense is marginal.** On offense alone the adjusted association is 0.88 (0.77–0.995), with a CI nearly touching 1. The effect is concentrated on defense (0.61).
4. **Success is weakly predictable from these observables.** Estimated P(success) barely varies across opportunities (AUC 0.59). The decision-value comparison is therefore driven mostly by correction value and option value, not by evidence about success. The real information set is unobservable.
5. **Raw rates at equal net value** barely differ by inventory. The adjusted gap depends on conditioning on inning, where one-challenge states concentrate.
6. **The "immediate exceeds preservation" share of passes depends on the information set.** It ranges from 61% under public information to 99.9% under the oracle.

### Evidence SUPPORTING it

1. **The association survives every specification tried.** It stays below 1 across all preservation policies, all four success models, both RE tables, exclusion of flagged rows, innings 1-8, score-dependent option value, team fixed effects and the near-boundary population not conditioned on correctness. The net-value model's OR ranges from 0.61 to 0.88 with every CI below 1. The one exception among the other models is offense-only with cost entered separately: 0.81 (0.64–1.01).
2. **No interaction with value in any variant.** Scarcity is not associated with a higher value threshold, but with a roughly uniform downshift. The one significant interaction (near-boundary, 0.85) points the other way.
3. **Most passes exceed the preservation benchmarks.** Estimated preservation costs are small compared with typical immediate EV: the median cost with one challenge left is 0.014 runs, against a median EV of 0.062. Under every non-oracle information set, most passes are of opportunities whose estimated immediate value exceeds every preservation benchmark.
4. **Unused challenges are the norm.** 74.9% of team-games end with a challenge unused, and 51% of all challenge units expire. Passes classed "immediate exceeds preservation" occur in those games at the same rate as other passes.

## Answers to the report questions

1. **Success estimability.** Poorly from these observables: chronological AUC 0.59 around a 53.8% base rate. The public-information incorrect-call rate is highly predictable (AUC 0.86), but challengers act on information these fields do not contain.
2. **Gross EV.** Median 0.063 runs, mean 0.082, P90 0.14, P99 0.34 (primary). This is about half the correction value, reflecting P(success) of about 0.55.
3. **Preservation value.** One challenge is worth about 0.08 runs at the start under league-typical future use, falling to 0.03 by the 7th and 0.01 by the 9th. Threshold-use benchmarks run about twice as high (0.17 falling to 0.03). A second challenge is worth about 0.02 runs early and near zero late.
4. **Expiration.** 74.9% of team-games end with a challenge unused, and 51.2% of all challenge units expire.
5. **One challenge after the fuller model.** Yes: OR 0.73 (0.67–0.80) net-value-adjusted, 0.80 (0.68–0.93) with cost entered separately.
6. **Threshold or reluctance.** Primarily generalized reluctance; no value-threshold signature.
7. **Value × scarcity after the fuller model.** No: OR 1.07 (0.77–1.47).
8. **Post-failure share.** In regulation, one-challenge status and failure are the same thing. Relative to teams with no prior success, the association is 0.78–0.88. Part of the one-vs-two gap comes from post-success elevation (1.22). When the player who failed is the decision-maker, the odds are 0.72.
9. **Passing opportunities that exceed preservation value.** Yes, under the benchmarks here: 85% of passes (61% under the public-information bound) have estimated immediate value above every preservation benchmark. Challenge rates rise strongly with net value, from 17% to 60% across the bins.
10. **Those passes and expiration.** 80.5% of them occurred in team-games that ended with an unused challenge, about the same as the 78.9% base rate for all passes.
11. **Offense vs defense.** The direction is the same on both sides. Magnitude differs: defense 0.61, offense 0.88 (CI barely excludes 1). The conclusions hold more strongly for defense.
12. **Braves.** See section 10: gross EV 0.33–0.49 runs vs preservation cost about 0.01 (at most 0.05). All three runner-state values fall in the "immediate exceeds preservation" class.
13. **Strongest evidence against.** Attenuation to 0.73-0.80 once cost is included. The post-success elevation, which means the one-vs-two gap is partly outcome-dependent activity. The marginal offense result. Weak observability of success.
14. **Strongest evidence for.** A robust sub-1 association in every specification, including a population not conditioned on correctness. No value interaction. Small preservation costs next to typical passed EV.
15. **Sufficiency.** See the recommendation in the final report.
