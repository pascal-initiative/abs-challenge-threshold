# Article 4 decision-value analysis — methodology

Decision-time economics of spending vs preserving an ABS challenge. This is research, not article prose, and not a challenge strategy. Everything is generated by `analyze.py` (core functions in `dv_core.py`, tested in `tests/test_article4_decision_value.py`).

## Inputs (read-only)

* Validated Article 4 dataset `research/article4/output`:
  * 20,164 eligible incorrect calls (10,755 offense, 9,409 defense);
  * all 21,831 incorrect calls;
  * team-game sequences;
  * RE288 table.

  The run asserts that input hashes match the Article 4 manifest.
* The processed Articles 1-3 pitch table (645,793 pitches) and all 9,485 official challenges. They supply geometry, pre-pitch state, inventory, and the population of called pitches used for the public-information benchmark.
* The immutable game feeds, for final scores only (expiration analysis).
* Primary run value: `correction_value_runs_pooled`, the pooled-count RE288 counterfactual correction value to the entitled team. The Sprint 5 smoothing value is a sensitivity. The 40 AMBIGUOUS counterfactual rows are excluded from value-based analyses.

## Leakage rules

1. **Cross-fitting.** Every model-based quantity attached to a row is predicted by a model fit **without that row's calendar month**. No row, and no game, contributes to its own prediction.
2. **Chronological evaluation.** Model quality is reported on expanding windows: train on months strictly before the test month, as in Articles 1-3. A chronological-only variant (April onward) is a sensitivity.
3. **Option value uses other games.** Future-opportunity and option values for a decision row are means over other months' games at the same decision state. They never use the same game's future.
4. **Hindsight is labelled.** Hindsight quantities (expiration, later maxima) appear only in `article4_expiration.csv` and `hindsight_*` columns of `article4_high_value_early.csv`, and never enter a model.

## What "decision-time information" means here

At the moment of the decision, the player does not know whether the call is wrong. The Hawk-Eye signed boundary distance determines ABS correctness exactly, so it is **never** used as a predictor. The models see two things:
* the **unsigned** distance from the ABS boundary (inches);
* the **nearest zone edge** (TOP, BOTTOM, INSIDE, OUTSIDE relative to the batter).

The nearest-edge class is identical whether the pitch is just inside or just outside that edge (unit-tested). Together, these describe how close and where the pitch was, not which side of the line it landed on. The player's actual information (perception, catcher's view, communication) is unobservable, so three information-set benchmarks bracket it:

| benchmark | P(challenge succeeds) estimated as | interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary: challenger-selected** | success rate of official challenges in the same observable state (all 9,485 challenges, successful and unsuccessful) | what challenging players in comparable situations achieved; includes their private information |
| Public information | rate at which the call was actually incorrect among **all** called pitches within 6 inches of the boundary with the same observables (185,688 chronological test pitches) | an uninformed challenger |
| Oracle | 1 for every eligible incorrect call | perfect knowledge that the call is wrong (the Article 4 dataset's implicit perspective) |

## Analysis 1: resource-independent challenge propensity

* **Target and population.** The target is `challenged` among eligible incorrect calls. This is observed behavior, not cognition. The name "resource-independent challenge propensity" is deliberate.
* **Specifications.** Four pre-declared specifications:
  * P1: distance spline + side;
  * P2: + nearest edge;
  * P3: + pitch family and handedness;
  * P4: + count, outs and inning.
* **Excluded.** Challenges remaining, prior challenge outcomes, and all future fields.
* **Selection rule.** The simplest specification within 0.001 of the best chronological log loss. The primary measure is restricted to evidence-only specifications (P1-P3) so that count/situation, which determine run value, enter the decision analysis separately. P4's performance is reported.

## Analysis 2: challenge success

* **Population.** All 9,485 official challenges; target OVERTURNED.
* **Specifications.**
  * S1: unsigned-distance spline × side;
  * S2: + nearest edge + terminal count;
  * S3: + pitch family and handedness;
  * S0: side + terminal only, a coarse sensitivity.
* **Selection.** The same selection rule as Analysis 1.
* **Reported.** Calibration by decile and probability bucket, performance by side and month, and the success curve by unsigned distance.

## Analysis 3: gross immediate expected value

`EV = P(success) × correction value`. Its distribution is reported, and challenge behavior is reported by inventory, inning, side, EV bucket and propensity quintile.

## Analyses 4-5: future opportunity stream and option value

* **Future stream.** For team T, every later incorrect call against T that a challenge could address: eligible or exhausted (the future stream does not depend on inventory). Position-player pitching is excluded. Each call carries its cross-fitted P(success), correction value and propensity.
* **Decision states.** (inning 1-9, 10 = extras pooled; half; outs; team home/away). For every team-game and every state reached, the realized future quantities are computed from the first pitch of that state. The 4,390 team-games give 226,586 team-game-state rows (113,293 game-states × 2 teams).
* **Expected values.** A decision row receives the **leave-one-month-out mean** over other months' team-games at its state. Game-cluster bootstrap 95% CIs (200 replicates) come from the full-sample state means.
* **Option value V_k.** Computed by exact backward recursion over each stream with the 2026 rules: a successful challenge is retained, and a failed one loses one unit. The recursion is verified against brute-force enumeration in the tests. Use policies are fixed benchmarks, not optimized:
  * `league_behavior` (primary): at each future opportunity, use a challenge with the cross-fitted resource-independent challenge propensity. This is the value of holding a challenge if future use resembles league behavior.
  * `tau_0.00`, `tau_0.05`, `tau_0.10`: use whenever future EV ≥ τ.
  * `prophet`: the single best future EV (one challenge), and the second-best as the marginal second challenge. Because successes are retained, threshold policies can exceed this value, so it is **not** an upper bound.

  V_0 = 0 in regulation. Extra-inning grants make V_0 > 0 in extras; extras rows (225) are excluded from classification and the main models.

## Analysis 6: spend vs preserve

* **Cost.** Preservation cost = P(fail) × [V_k − V_{k−1}], the expected loss of option value if this challenge fails.
* **Net value.** Net decision value = EV − cost, using the league-behavior policy.
* **Classes.**
  * IMMEDIATE_EXCEEDS_PRESERVATION: EV > the highest cost across all policies, using each policy's upper 95% CI.
  * PRESERVATION_EXCEEDS_IMMEDIATE: EV < the lowest cost using the lower CI.
  * OVERLAPPING: otherwise.
* **Behavior models.** Logistic models of `challenged`, game-clustered:
  * M0: the exploratory model A;
  * M1: + gross EV spline + propensity logit;
  * M2: + preservation cost;
  * M3: net-value spline;
  * M1i/M3i: inventory × EV/net interactions;
  * M4: + team fixed effects.

  Controls: side, inning bucket, score state, late-and-close.

## Analysis 7: post-failure

In regulation innings, a team can hold one challenge only after exactly one failed challenge. Inventory 1 and "has failed" are therefore **the same state**; the only other one-challenge route is an extra-inning grant.

States:
* two left, no prior success;
* two left after a prior success;
* one left, by recency of the failure (≤3, 4-12, >12 plate appearances);
* one left from an extra-inning grant (described, not modelled, because it only occurs in extras).

Within the one-challenge rows, the models also test recency, whether the failure was on the same side, and whether the same player failed.

## Analysis 8: expiration (HINDSIGHT)

Final inventory per team-game is taken from the last pitch's pre-pitch inventory, adjusted if that pitch had a confirmed challenge. Unused units are counted against 2 per team-game plus extra-inning grants. Results are stratified by final inventory, home/away, close game (final margin ≤ 2), extra innings and team.

## Analysis 9: high-value early cohort

Unchallenged eligible calls in innings 1-3 worth ≥0.50 runs (n = 126, the exploratory cohort), with the full decision-time decomposition and hindsight columns.

## Analysis 10: Braves

Pitch 824887:3:7. Decision-time inputs are the recorded state only; the three validated counterfactual correction values are carried through the same framework.

## Analysis 11: sensitivity and falsification

The scarcity models are refit under:
* each preservation policy;
* the oracle, coarse, public-information and chronological-only success models;
* the Sprint 5 RE table;
* exclusion of unrecorded-runner-action rows;
* offense only and defense only;
* innings 1-8;
* score-bucket option-value states;
* a **near-boundary population not conditioned on call correctness**: every called pitch within 3 inches with inventory available, using flip value computed from count/base-out state. Flip value reproduces the Article 4 R1 value exactly: max difference 0.

## Limitations that bound interpretation

* The player's information set is unobservable. The primary success model conditions on challenger selection.
* The option-value policies are benchmarks, not an optimal policy. The league-behavior benchmark inherits whatever behavior teams exhibit.
* The future stream excludes correct calls a team might challenge in the future, which would add failure risk and lower option value.
* In regulation, one-challenge status and prior failure cannot be separated.
* The data are observational. No association here is causal, and no label of optimal, irrational or wasteful behavior is warranted.
