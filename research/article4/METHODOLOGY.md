# Article 4 methodology — what is a challenge worth?

This is a research-dataset methodology, not an article. It describes how the Article 4 artifacts are built and what they cannot show. No inferential modeling, win-probability work, or interpretation is performed. Numbers cited here come from `output/VALIDATION_REPORT.md`, which is generated by `build.py`.

## 1. Source data

| Source | Use | Location |
|---|---|---|
| Accepted Articles 1-3 snapshot, 2026-03-25 to 2026-09-09 (2,195 games, 645,793 pitches, 9,485 official challenges) | Population, pre-pitch state (Statcast), geometry, calls, challenges, inventory | `data/full_season/processed/pitches.csv`, `challenges.csv`, `validation_report.json` |
| Immutable MLB game feeds, the exact objects referenced by `source_game_feed` | Runner movements, linked baserunning actions, runs to the end of the half-inning, play text | `data/full_season/raw/objects/<sha256>` via `receipts.jsonl` |
| Sprint 3 (Articles 1-2) population builder and temporal predictions | Population reproduction (`src.sprint3.build_population`); held-out expected recognition | `src/sprint3.py`, `data/analysis/sprint3/offensive_recognition_predictions.csv` |
| Sprint 4 (Article 3) opportunity expectations | Contextual expected recognition probability | `data/analysis/sprint4/batter_recognition_opportunities.csv` |
| Post-snapshot Braves feeds (case study only) | Braves case-study search only; never enters populations, RE, or summaries | `output/case_study_raw/` (receipts + content-addressed objects) |

Nothing is re-acquired for the analysis itself. `build.py` hashes 352 protected Article 1-3 files before and after the run and fails if any changed.

## 2. Population and joins

* **OFFENSE (primary, Articles 1-3 population).** Called STRIKE whose fixed ABS geometry (`abs_2026_circle_rectangle_r1.45in_v1`) is BALL, with a legal offensive challenge available. It is produced by calling the unchanged `build_population` and asserting 10,755 opportunities and 2,112 challenges. The set of eligible OFFENSE pitch keys must equal the Articles 1-3 feature table exactly.
* **DEFENSE (extension).** Called BALL whose geometry is STRIKE, with a legal defensive challenge available. The same eligibility rule is applied: available, not resource-exhausted, not position-player pitching, not unknown. It reconciles to the Sprint 3 defensive summary (9,409 / 2,986). It is included because challenge inventory is shared by a team's batters and batterymates, the Braves case is a defensive opportunity, and several brief examples (ball four that should be strike three) are defensive. Filter `opportunity_side == "OFFENSE"` to use only the Articles 1-3 population.
* **Ineligible incorrect calls** (exhausted, position-player pitching) are kept in `article4_all_incorrect_calls.csv` and in the team-game sequences, so that "a later opportunity occurred while exhausted" can be measured. They are not opportunities.
* **Join keys.** `pitch_key` (`game_pk:at_bat_index:play_event_index`) is the Articles 1-3 key and joins every prior artifact. Feed replay uses the same key. Every eligible row joined one-to-one; the build fails on any duplicate or missing feed state.
* **Geometry.** OFFENSE rows keep the Articles 1-3 fields (`miss_axis`, `miss_side`, distances). For DEFENSE rows the pitch is inside the zone, so "distance outside" is undefined. `abs_distance_inches` is the distance inside the zone, and `miss_side` names the nearest edge (`NEAREST_TOP`, etc.). No established metric is redefined.

## 3. State reconstruction from the feed

`feeds.py` replays each feed in event order.

* **Pre-pitch state:** count from the previous event's count in the plate appearance (so automatic balls and strikes are included); outs from runner-movement outs; base occupancy by replaying movements keyed by their `start` base (robust to pinch-runner ID changes); extra-inning placed runners from `runner_placed` events; score from scoring movements. Agreement with the independent Statcast pre-pitch state in the processed table is 1.00000 on every field.
* **Linked events.** An event is attached to a pitch only by a source field:
  * baserunning `action` events whose `actionPlayId` equals the pitch `playId`;
  * the immediately following `pickoff` event when `fromCatcher = true`;
  * runner movements whose `playIndex` is the pitch or a linked event.

  No timing heuristics are used.
* **Actual post-pitch state** = pre-pitch state plus the linked movements, plus the recorded call's count consequence.
* **Independent check.** The reconstructed actual post-pitch state is compared with the Statcast pre-pitch state of the next pitch in the game. Differences explained by recorded, state-changing non-pitch events (automatic ball/strike, intentional walk, balk, pickoff) are labelled as such. The only unexplained cases are three catcher-interference plays, which the engine already flags as ambiguous (R5).

## 4. Counterfactual rules

For every incorrect call, the engine (`counterfactual.py`, unit-tested in `tests/test_article4_counterfactual.py`) receives four inputs:

* the pre-pitch state;
* the call the feed recorded (post-challenge);
* the alternative call;
* the linked movements.

It returns the actual state and the alternative state. **Observed** means the state if the umpire's incorrect original call stands. **Corrected** means the state if ABS corrects it. For overturned challenges the feed recorded the corrected outcome, so the observed state is the reconstructed one (`observed_state_is_reconstructed`).

Movements are classified as batter, walk-force award, live-ball runner action (steal, caught stealing, wild pitch, passed ball, defensive indifference, error, runner out, pickoff), or unsupported.

| Rule | Condition | Treatment | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1_NO_RUNNER_ACTION | No live-ball runner movement linked to the pitch | Apply the alternative call mechanically: count change; walk with force chain and forced run; strikeout with added out; a third out ends the half-inning | EXACT |
| R2_RUNNER_OUTCOME_STANDS | Runner action; neither call ends the plate appearance | Keep the runner's live-ball result; change the count only | RULE_BASED |
| R3_TERMINAL_CALL_RUNNER_ACTION | Runner action; either call ends the plate appearance (ball four / strike three) | No primary state. Enumerate two bounds: runner result stands; runners returned to the bases held at the time of the pitch | AMBIGUOUS |
| R4_UNCAUGHT_THIRD_STRIKE | Alternative call is strike three and the pitch was not caught cleanly (wild pitch, passed ball, ball in dirt) | Enumerate batter out / batter reaches first, crossed with R3 bounds | AMBIGUOUS |
| R5_UNSUPPORTED_MOVEMENT | Catcher interference, "other advance", or movements that do not reconcile | No state | AMBIGUOUS |

**Rule basis.** Published 2026 guidance says the outcome of a stolen-base attempt stands whatever the challenge result, except when an overturned call is ball four or strike three. In that case the umpire may place runners, and any doubt is resolved toward the last base legally touched at the time of the challenge ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/mlb/story/_/id/47914496/mlb-unveils-abs-challenge-system-guidelines-2026-season), [Yahoo Sports](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/abs-challenges-2026-mlb-rule-185802098.html)). The archived MLB announcement PDF confirms the inventory rules (two per team, successful challenges retained, one granted in each extra inning if none remain) but not runner placement. R2 follows the stolen-base guidance. R3 does not guess the umpire's discretion.

Additional deterministic conventions:
* A strikeout that makes the third out voids runs on the same pitch, because the out occurs when the pitch is caught.
* A game-ending (walk-off) state sets the future run expectancy to 0 and is flagged.

## 5. Run-expectancy valuation

* **RE288 (primary).** For every pitch in innings 1-8 of half-innings that ended with three outs (583,019 pitches), the target is runs scored from that pitch to the end of the half-inning, taken from replayed feed movements. Replayed half-inning runs match the linescore in 39,046 of 39,048 halves; the two exceptions are halves the linescore does not record. The state is (balls, strikes, outs, bases). The cell mean is smoothed toward the base-out mean with a 40-pitch prior, which is the Sprint 5 convention reused unchanged. `article4_re288_table.csv` reports n, half-innings, SD, raw and smoothed values for all 288 states (minimum n = 7).
* **Why not Sprint 5's RE directly.** Sprint 5 summed `result.rbi` per half-inning, which omits runs scored on wild pitches, errors, balks, and similar plays. Article 4 counts scoring movements instead. This is documented, not retrofitted into Sprint 5.
* **Values.**
  * `RE_observed` = runs on the pitch + RE288(observed post-pitch state).
  * `RE_corrected` is defined the same way for the corrected state.
  * `RE_delta_batting = RE_observed - RE_corrected`, from the batting-team perspective. It is positive when the uncorrected call favored the batting team (typical for DEFENSE rows) and negative for OFFENSE rows.
  * `RE_delta_fielding = -RE_delta_batting`.
  * `correction_value_runs` is the value of correction to the team entitled to challenge.
  * `challenge_value_runs` is realized: the correction value if overturned, 0 if confirmed.
  * `missed_challenge_value_runs` is the unchallenged eligible value.
  * Realized later runs are never used.
* **Sensitivities.**
  * Leave-one-month-out tables (`*_lomo`) check that the game's own month does not drive values: mean |difference| 0.010 runs.
  * A pooled-count table (`*_pooled`: base-out mean plus a count effect pooled across base states within outs).
* **Methodology flaw found and documented, not worked around.** The primary smoothed RE288 violates count ordering in 25 of 216 extra-ball comparisons and 15 of 192 extra-strike comparisons. The violations are all in sparse cells, for example a 3-0 count with a runner on third. As a result, 82 eligible rows have a small negative primary correction value (−0.012 to −0.085 runs). Runner events are held equal between the two states, so the correct call cannot truly cost the entitled team: these values are estimation artifacts. They are flagged `re_ordering_violation`. The pooled-count table has no ordering violations and no negative values. Its values differ from the primary by 0.039 runs on average. Which table becomes primary for Article 4 analysis is a decision for the analysts; the primary here stays the pre-specified Sprint 5 convention.
* **Environment and leakage.** The RE table is a season-level environment parameter estimated on the whole snapshot. The leave-one-month-out variant shows this has negligible influence. Decision-time fields never use it to encode future game events.

## 6. Challenge inventory reconstruction

The Articles 1-3 sequential inventory (`affected_team_challenges_remaining`) is retained. It is also recomputed independently from `challenges.csv`: start at 2, subtract one per CONFIRMED challenge, retain after OVERTURNED, and grant one at the start of an extra inning if none remain. The recomputation agrees on every pitch in the snapshot. Prior-challenge history fields count only strictly earlier pitches in the game. A 2,000-row brute-force recomputation found 0 mismatches.

## 7. Team-game sequences and summaries

`article4_team_game_sequences.csv` lists, for each team-game in chronological order, every event that bears on the team's challenge resources:

* eligible incorrect calls the team could challenge;
* incorrect calls against the team while exhausted or while a position player pitched;
* challenges of calls the geometry says were correct, which still consume inventory.

Inventory before and after each row reconciles chronologically. All 9,485 official challenges appear. Fields prefixed `hindsight_` summarize later events in the same game, such as whether a more valuable opportunity came later and whether it was challenged. They exist so the brief's question 6 can be asked. They must never be used as decision-time predictors, and a missing later opportunity does not imply the earlier hold was intentional.

`article4_team_summary.csv` is purely descriptive. It covers opportunities, challenge rates, realized and missed values, and expected recognition, by team, side, inning bucket and inventory, plus league rows. Ambiguous-value opportunities are counted but excluded from value sums.

## 8. Competing-attention classification

Raw source fields are preserved:
* linked event types and descriptions;
* movements;
* fielding credits on runner movements;
* catcher-pickoff flag;
* replay review on linked actions;
* the next event.

Derived `ca_*` indicators are simple deterministic functions of these fields and are defined in the data dictionary. Important observability limits:

* A catcher throw is recorded only when it produces a credit: an assist, error, touch, or a catcher pickoff. A successful steal carries no throw credit, so `ca_catcher_throw_recorded = False` does not mean no throw.
* **Plays on forced runners are erased by a walk.** When ball four is called, a forced runner's steal attempt, the catcher's throw, and any out call are nullified and do not appear in the feed, in Statcast, or in the ABS extract. The Braves case below is a confirmed example. Rows where the recorded outcome is a walk with a forced runner and no linked action are flagged `cf_unrecorded_runner_action_possible` (174 eligible rows, 39 of the 250 high-value missed rows). For those rows the R1 counterfactual is exact only with respect to the record, and competing-attention indicators undercount.
* No broadcast, timing, or tracking data on player attention exists in these sources. The indicators measure recorded concurrent baserunning events, not attention.

## 9. Braves case study

The recollection supplied in the brief could not be matched: no 2026 Braves game through 2026-09-23 ended 5-1. The user then identified the game as the 2026-08-10 Mets at Braves game. Pitch `824887:3:7` matches every recorded element of the revised account:
* one out, Bichette on first, 3-2 count, batter Carson Benge, pitcher Bryce Elder;
* called ball four on a pitch inside the ABS zone 2.7 inches below the top edge;
* no recorded challenge;
* Baty's grand slam three batters later for a 5-0 lead.

The steal attempt, the throw, any second-base umpire call, and Elder's challenge request are absent from every archived source. The final score was 8-5, not 5-1.

`output/article4_braves_case_summary.json` values the decision point three ways without choosing among them:
* the recorded-data counterfactual (strikeout, runner stays on first);
* the runner ruled safe at second;
* the runner ruled out, ending the inning.

The realized four-run grand slam is listed only as a contrast, never as the cost of the missed correction.

## 10. Exclusions, ambiguities, limitations

* Position-player-pitching and resource-exhausted incorrect calls are not opportunities; they are kept in `article4_all_incorrect_calls.csv` and the sequences. No eligible row is dropped.
* 40 eligible rows (0.2%) are AMBIGUOUS; they carry bounds (`correction_value_runs_lower/upper`), not a primary value.
* Recognition probabilities exist only for OFFENSE rows. The Article 3 probability is an in-sample full-season fit; a held-out temporal probability is provided alongside (March has none). No new recognition model was fitted for DEFENSE rows.
* No leverage index is manufactured (consistent with Article 2); `late_close` and score/state fields are provided.
* An unchallenged incorrect call is not evidence of non-recognition or of deliberate conservation. These data cannot distinguish recognition failure, conservation, or execution/timing failure. The Braves case shows a timing/eligibility failure that the sources do not record at all.
* ABS geometry, Statcast locations, and feed coding carry measurement error. Sources may be revised after archiving.
* The dataset is observational, season-to-date, and descriptive.
